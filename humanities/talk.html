“Fixity”

The printing press (~1450) made identical copies of a text at scale.

Science adopted the same form: Philosophical Transactions (1665) made the paper the fixed record of a finding.

E. Eisenstein, The Printing Press as an Agent of Change (1979). Images: Wikimedia Commons.
“You’d think they were speaking as if they had some understanding, but if you question anything that has been said because you want to learn more, it continues to signify just that very same thing forever.”
Plato, Phaedrus
Socrates said this about writing around 370 BCE. Print made it true at scale, and web search made fixed documents easy to find but left them fixed. Chat is the first time text can answer back.

Ballot Measures Are Hard to Read

Ballot measures are proposals put to a public vote. They are written in complex legal language → voter misunderstanding and disengagement (Reilly and Richey 2011; Burnett and Kogan 2015; Ballotpedia 2026).
Concurrent work: Ash, Galletta, and Opocher, BallotBot.
Democracy depends on an informed electorate; technology has sometimes worked against this (filter bubbles, echo chambers).
Can AI-based chat help voters make more informed decisions?

We chose two measures that differ in topic, complexity, and likely ideological valence.

A Quick Overview of the Current Results

  • Minimum wages (Cengiz et al. 2019), to 2024: no clear job loss through the $15 era.
  • Unemployment (Shimer 2012), to 2026: unemployment still rises mainly because people take longer to find new jobs, not because more people lose jobs. The exception is 2020, when COVID layoffs drove the spike.
  • Regional economies (Blanchard and Katz 1992), to 2025: migration now absorbs less of a local job loss.
  • China Syndrome (Autor, Dorn, and Hanson 2013), to 2023: most of the manufacturing job loss had not recovered by 2023.
  • Allocation of talent (Hsieh et al. 2019), to 2024: falling barriers now add 4–8% of growth, down from about 40% in 1960–2010.
